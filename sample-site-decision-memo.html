pggLLC / Site & LIHTC Readiness ReviewNovember 26, 2026 · Fruita city, ColoradoTo: Board of Cedar Grove Community Trust (fictional)

Cedar Grove Community Trust is fictional. For this example only, assume it controls an unspecified property in Fruita and wants to explore affordable rental housing while retaining land control. No actual parcel, client, zoning designation, unit count, budget, or development proposal has been assessed.

Preliminary recommendation

Advance conditionally to limited due diligence.

Advance conditionally to scoping and verification. The public evidence supports investigating an affordable rental concept, especially the ability to serve lower-income renters. It does not establish that this unspecified property can support a project. Retain land control and stop short of a design, unit-yield, rent, or funding commitment.

Decision requested

Authorize staff and pggLLC to define a limited first-stage investigation, request a City pre-application conversation, assemble existing property records, and obtain priced consultant scopes. This sample authorizes no land disposition, developer selection, exclusivity, debt, tax-credit application, construction, or third-party expenditure. The board must approve any paid investigation at the next gate.

What the public evidence says

Geography is Fruita city (GEOID 0828745), not all of Mesa County. COHO reports population 13,691 and median household income $87,184. These figures and the four measures below use ACS 2020-2024 five-year inputs; the 103-rental result is COHO-calculated. The gap also uses COHO’s HUD-based AMI thresholds; the current overview labels FY2026 income limits. It is not an official program eligibility result. The digest was generated November 22, 2026 and retrieved November 26, 2026. Survey estimates describe a pooled period, not current listings or a November 2026 census.

43.6%

Renter cost burden

COHO reports this share of Fruita renters paying over 30% of income toward housing.

Decision implication: Prioritize affordability and operating support in the program discussion; this is not a count of households who will lease a new project.

$1,472 / month

Median gross rent

COHO reports the ACS median gross rent for Fruita; gross rent includes contract rent and tenant-paid utility costs.

Decision implication: Use as historical market context, not a new-construction achievable rent, a bedroom-specific comparison, or a LIHTC rent ceiling.

20.1%

Renter-occupied share

Share of occupied Fruita homes reported as renter occupied.

Decision implication: Evaluate rental options alongside local housing mix; tenure alone does not prove demand for a particular design.

103 rentals

Price-only gap at <=30% AMI

COHO calculates 295 renter households against 192 rentals priced for that income level.

Decision implication: This modeled shortage supports further investigation. It counts rentals regardless of who occupies them; it is not a vacancy count, an available-unit gap, a waiting list, or a target project size.

At the historical median gross rent, a simple 30%-of-income illustration is $1,472 x 12 / 0.30 = $58,880 per year. This is arithmetic, not an eligibility rule. The $87,184 city median covers all households and must not be substituted for renter income or HUD program AMI. HUD limits, household size, bedroom count, and utility allowances must be checked separately.

Key unresolved issues

The site has not been identified or screened. These are required investigations, not findings.

Land control & title

Lead: Nonprofit staff + counsel

Evidence needed: Title, survey availability, easements, restrictions, options, and grant or donor conditions.

Decision test: Confirm the nonprofit can investigate the property without compromising its mission or control.

Zoning & approval route

Lead: pggLLC + City staff

Evidence needed: Written pre-application record: allowed uses, review route, dimensional standards, parking, access, required studies and hearing steps.

Decision test: Identify whether a workable concept is permitted or requires discretionary approvals; do not assume density.

Utilities, access & civil

Lead: Civil engineer + providers

Evidence needed: Service and capacity inquiries, access constraints, off-site work, drainage and preliminary cost-risk range.

Decision test: Pause or redesign if a critical access or capacity issue has no credible solution.

Environmental & physical

Lead: Qualified specialists

Evidence needed: Appropriate environmental review scope, floodplain and other hazard checks, soils/geotechnical needs and survey scope.

Decision test: Resolve fatal flaws before commissioning a full design.

Demand, program & operations

Lead: Market analyst + operator

Evidence needed: Defined market area, existing/approved pipeline, comparable rents and concessions, unit/bedroom needs, services and operating costs.

Decision test: Commission a formal study where required; public screening is not an absorption or rent conclusion.

Capital & partnership

Lead: Housing finance adviser + counsel

Evidence needed: Preliminary sources/uses, land treatment, subsidy needs, lender/equity assumptions, partner roles, guarantees and control provisions.

Decision test: Return with a plausible structure and downside cases before any partner or land commitment.

Funding and LIHTC fit

Keep LIHTC as a path to test, not a conclusion. A qualified finance team should compare the applicable competitive-credit and bond-financed routes, scale, transaction costs, current CHFA allocation requirements, and likely subsidy needs. Confirm the correct program year, income and rent limits, utility allowances, eligibility, site-control terms, application timing, market-study requirements, and any location-based criteria. No award probability, basis boost, tax treatment, or allocation is assumed. A nominal land contribution cannot substitute for a tested capital and operating plan.

Alternatives

Advance in stages (recommended)

Preserve the affordable rental option while resolving fatal flaws first.

Use only if staff can define a bounded investigation and retain control.

Revise the concept or partnership

Consider a different scale, tenure, resident focus, land structure, or development partner.

Use if site constraints or mission/operating needs point to a better fit.

Pause or pursue another site

Protect time and land value rather than extending an unsupported concept.

Use if legal, access, infrastructure, environmental, market, or funding risks cannot be bounded.

Authorized next actions and decision gates

Set the calendar and return dates when the board approves the scope. These are proposed gates, not promised processing timelines.

Gate 1

Scope gate

Accountable lead: Nonprofit lead + pggLLC

Return with: Existing records, City questions, consultant proposals, responsibility map.

Approval condition: Board approves a written scope, spending cap and return date before paid third-party work.

Gate 2

Fatal-flaw gate

Accountable lead: pggLLC + counsel + civil/environmental specialists

Return with: Title/control review, pre-application record, access/service response, critical risk register.

Approval condition: Advance only with no unresolved fatal flaw and an agreed plan for remaining risks; otherwise revise or pause.

Gate 3

Program & finance gate

Accountable lead: Market analyst + finance adviser + operator

Return with: Demand evidence, realistic program, operating assumptions, preliminary capital structure and sensitivities.

Approval condition: Board decides whether to fund formal studies and a development path; no commitment based solely on COHO figures.

Gate 4

Land / partner commitment gate

Accountable lead: Board + counsel

Return with: Competitive/appropriate partner process, negotiated control terms and complete decision record.

Approval condition: Separate approval for an option, ground lease, sale, exclusivity, developer agreement or financing. Retain land control until approved.

Land-control protection and proposed board direction

Approve preparation of a scoped affordable-rental feasibility investigation for the hypothetical Fruita property. Direct staff to retain land control, seek written jurisdiction and utility information, and return with priced scopes, a proposed spending cap, a decision timetable, and a risk register. Require separate board approval before third-party expenditures, developer selection, exclusivity, land disposition or financing. Permit an advance, revise, or pause recommendation at every gate.

Evidence limits and source notes

COHO is a public-data screening resource, not a parcel study, appraisal, formal market study, underwriting, legal opinion, or CHFA determination. Margins of error for the selected ACS estimates are not supplied in the cited digest; significance is not established. The memo relies only on consistent figures checked between COHO’s rendered overview and its public digest. Its <=60% AMI gap differs between those views (232 displayed versus 204 in the digest), so that measure is excluded pending reconciliation. Different ACS and HUD CHAS vintages and price-only versus affordable-and-available definitions must not be blended.
